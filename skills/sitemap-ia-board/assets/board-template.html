Jargon Dictionary

Every technical term used in this document, in plain language.

Marketing & Conversion

B2B / B2C — Business-to-business (selling to companies) vs business-to-consumer (selling to individuals).
CRO — Conversion Rate Optimization: designing pages so more visitors take action (inquire, book, buy) instead of just browsing and leaving.
Conversion / conversion event — The moment a visitor does what we want: submits a form, taps the contact button, downloads a guide. Each one is tracked in analytics.
CTA — Call To Action: the button or link telling the visitor what to do next.
Funnel — The path a visitor takes from first visit to becoming a lead or customer.
Landing page — A focused page where ad traffic "lands", stripped of distractions so visitors either convert or leave.
Lead — A potential customer who shared their contact details through any form or channel.
Lead magnet — Something valuable given free in exchange for contact details (a guide, a calculator result, a discount).
Gated content — Content you can only get after entering your email — that's what makes it capture leads.
Lead nurturing — Following up captured leads over time (emails, calls) until they're ready to buy.
Lead scoring / qualification — Judging how serious a lead is from what they told us, so sales calls the hottest ones first.
Social proof — Evidence that others trust us: client logos, testimonials, reviews, case studies.
UTM parameters — Tags added to links in ads/posts so we know which campaign brought each lead.
CRM — Customer Relationship Management system: where sales tracks every lead and deal.

SEO (search visibility)

SEO — Search Engine Optimization: making the site appear high on Google for relevant searches — free traffic, long-term.
H1 — The main headline of a page. Google weighs it heavily, so it must contain the words people search for.
Geo intent / long-tail queries — Searches tied to a place or very specific needs. Less traffic each, but high buying intent.
hreflang — A hidden tag telling Google which language versions of a page exist, so each user sees the right one.
Schema / structured data — Hidden labels in the code (Product, LocalBusiness) that let Google show rich extras: ratings, prices, map cards.
Rich results — Enhanced Google listings that schema unlocks — they occupy more screen and get more clicks. Note: Google discontinued FAQ rich results for most sites in 2023.
Canonical — Telling Google which page is the "official" version when similar content exists in several places.
Indexable filter URLs — Making filtered catalog views real pages Google can rank, instead of invisible filter states.
NAP — Name, Address, Phone. Must be written identically on the website and Google listing.
Google Business Profile (GMB) — The map card with hours, photos and reviews when someone googles the business.
Local SEO — Ranking for searches in a specific area ("near me" queries).
XML sitemap — A machine-readable list of all pages, handed to Google so nothing gets missed. (Different from this planning document.)
Core Web Vitals — Google's speed and stability scores. Slow sites rank lower and lose impatient visitors.
Thin content — Pages with too little substance; Google penalizes them.

UX & Design

UX — User Experience: how easy and pleasant the site is to use, with minimum confusion.
IA (Information Architecture) — How content is organized and connected across pages — what this document defines.
Persona — A named stand-in for one type of buyer, built from evidence about who actually buys. Its job is to settle arguments about the structure: if a page serves no persona, it is being built on taste.
Finding id (f-…) — The reference to one item in the site audit. The card shows the id, not the audit's wording, so the two documents cannot drift apart when a finding is reworded.
Sitemap — The map of all pages and how they link together (the board above).
Above the fold — What's visible before any scrolling. The most valuable real estate on a page.
Hero — The big top section of a page: headline, image, primary buttons.
Breadcrumb — The small path line like Home › Category › Item — shows where you are, helps Google understand structure.
Faceted filters — Multiple filters combinable at once to narrow a catalog quickly.
Multi-step form — A long form split into small steps with a progress bar. Feels easier, completes more often.
Sticky (navbar / bar) — An element that stays on screen while scrolling, keeping key actions one tap away.
Click-to-chat / click-to-call — Buttons that open a chat app or dial directly on tap.
RTL — Right-To-Left: languages like Arabic mirror the entire layout — menus, arrows, alignment — not just the text.
Wayfinding — Anything helping visitors know where they are and where to go next.

Technical & Database

CMS — Content Management System: the admin panel where the client edits content without a developer.
Headless CMS — A CMS that only stores content and serves it via API; the front-end is built separately.
Table / collection — A structured list in the database. Same idea as a spreadsheet tab.
FK (Foreign Key) — A field linking one table to another.
M:1 / M:N relations — How tables connect: many-to-one (many items, one category) vs many-to-many.
Nullable — A field allowed to stay empty.
Enum — A field restricted to fixed choices.
Slug — The readable end of a URL. Each language gets its own.
Locale / field-level localization — Locale = a language version. Field-level = one record holds all languages side by side, instead of duplicating everything.
Singleton — A table with exactly one record — site settings exist once, not as a list.
JSON payload — A flexible data blob holding form answers that vary by form type.
Seed — The starter records entered when the database is created.
Webhook / API — How systems talk automatically: a webhook pushes each new lead to the CRM the second it's submitted.
Migration — Restructuring a live database later. Risky and costly — why Phase 2 tables are created empty now.
GA4 — Google Analytics 4: the free tool tracking visits, sources and conversion events.
SLA — Service Level Agreement: a promised standard, e.g. delivery or response times.
